# ICSR-reporting-
A 24-year-old female patient (60 kg) with a history of myasthenia gravis was admitted and started on intravenous piperacillin–tazobactam (1500 mg) on 15 December 2026 for treatment of an infection. Approximately four days after initiation of therapy, the patient developed painful ulcerations involving both the buccal mucosa and lips, along with erythematous papules and target-like lesions over the trunk and limbs. These findings were clinically suggestive of erythema multiforme, a probable hypersensitivity reaction to the antibiotic. Piperacillin–tazobactam was discontinued immediately, and the patient was treated with supportive medications, including corticosteroids, antihistamines, topical therapy, and appropriate symptomatic management. Following withdrawal of the suspected drug and initiation of treatment, the patient's condition improved. Based on the temporal relationship between drug administration and symptom onset, improvement after drug withdrawal, and clinical assessment, the reaction was classified as a probable adverse drug reaction (ADR).
